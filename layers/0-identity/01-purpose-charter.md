## Primary Purpose

:::rcos{clauses="2.1.1, 2.1.2, 2.1.5"}
:::

:::rationale{title="Why a single enduring purpose?"}
Every decision, role, and allocation of resources downstream has to stay consistent with one thing — the community's primary purpose. If it drifts, shifts with trends, or gets diluted by competing goals, the community loses coherence.
:::

_<The community's primary purpose — to be defined.>_

## Secondary Purposes

:::rcos{clauses="2.1.4"}
:::

:::rationale{title="Why allow secondary purposes?"}
A community rarely does only one thing. Secondary purposes make space for the other concrete outcomes the community pursues — but they are subordinate. If a secondary purpose ever conflicts with the primary purpose, the primary purpose takes precedence.
:::

_<Secondary purposes — to be defined.>_

## Non-Goals and Exclusions

:::rationale{title="Why state what the community is not?"}
Communities drift by accretion — one uncontested assumption at a time. Naming what the community is explicitly _not_ makes boundary violations visible early, and gives anyone a clear basis to object when scope creep begins.
:::

_<Non-goals and explicit exclusions — to be defined.>_

## Conditions for Purpose Change

:::rcos{clauses="2.1.3"}
:::

:::rationale{title="Why make changing the purpose hard?"}
Purpose is the one thing everything else depends on. If it were easy to change, nothing above it in the stack — membership, governance, invariants — could be trusted to mean the same thing from one decision cycle to the next.
:::

_<Conditions and decision type required to change the primary purpose — to be defined.>_
