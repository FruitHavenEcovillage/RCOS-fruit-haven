# Scope Declaration

- **Layer:** 0 — Identity & Scope
- **Status:** Present
- **RCOS reference:** §2.2, §2.5

---

## In-Scope Assets

:::rcos{clauses="2.2.1, 2.2.2, 2.2.4"}
:::

:::rationale{title="Why enumerate every governed asset?"}
If the community has not explicitly named an asset as in-scope, it isn't — full stop. Listing assets by name closes the gap where informal claims of authority grow over undeclared resources, and prevents scope creep.
:::

1. Fruit Haven's governing documents — the [FH1](/proposals/passed/2019-05-17_fh1-bylaws) and [FH3](/proposals/passed/2019-05-17_fh3-bylaws) Community Bylaws, the [Community Sovereignty Doctrine](/proposals/passed/2019-05-17_federation-sovereignty), and their RCOS-mapped artifacts in this repository
2. The shared treasury, as defined in the [Treasury Ruleset](/layers/3-economy/02-treasury-ruleset) (FH1 Bylaws Art. 9)
3. The FH1 property (~26.4359 hectares, Sector Las Peñas, Ecuador) — its Community Area, Private Homestead Areas, and Nature Reserve as allotted under the [Land Allotment Protocol](/layers/3-economy/03-land-allotment-protocol) (FH1 Bylaws Art. 7)
4. _<Reserved — digital platforms, websites, and hosted services (e.g. fruithaven.land) are not yet named as in-scope assets in any ratified bylaws or doctrine. Pending future ratification.>_
5. The "Fruit Haven" name and title, as referenced in the Community Sovereignty Doctrine (Art. IV(b))
6. Community areas of other Fruit Haven properties and developing communities within the federation, per the [Federation Protocol](/layers/2-governance/04-federation-protocol)

## In-Scope Decision Domains

:::rcos{clauses="2.2.1, 2.2.2"}
:::

:::rationale{title="Why name decision domains, not just assets?"}
Scope isn't only about stuff — it's about which kinds of questions the community gets to answer collectively. Naming decision domains makes it unambiguous where collective authority applies and where it doesn't.
:::

1. Governance rules and decision processes (Layer 2), per FH1 Bylaws Art. 11
2. Membership rules, states, and admission (Layer 1), per FH1 Bylaws Art. 8 and the Community Sovereignty Doctrine Art. II
3. Treasury and shared resource allocation (Layer 3), per FH1 Bylaws Art. 9
4. Fruit Haven's governing documents (bylaws, doctrine, and their RCOS-mapped artifacts) and their evolution, per FH1 Bylaws Art. 5 (decentralization review) and Art. 6 (bylaw amendment process)
5. _<Reserved — governance of Fruit Haven-owned platforms, websites, and public channels is not yet defined as a decision domain in any ratified bylaws or doctrine. Pending future ratification.>_
6. Support and facilitation offered to developing Fruit Haven communities (meetings, guidance, resources), per the Community Sovereignty Doctrine Art. V and the [Federation Protocol](/layers/2-governance/04-federation-protocol)
7. Partnerships, external collaborations, and use of the "Fruit Haven" name, per the Community Sovereignty Doctrine Art. IV

## In-Scope Activities and Responsibilities

:::rcos{clauses="2.2.1, 2.2.2"}
:::

:::rationale{title="Why declare the work the community owns?"}
Authority without owned responsibility produces paralysis; responsibility without declared authority produces burnout and blame. Listing the activities the community collectively governs makes the match explicit.
:::

1. Maintaining and evolving Fruit Haven's governing documents — the [FH1](/proposals/passed/2019-05-17_fh1-bylaws) and [FH3](/proposals/passed/2019-05-17_fh3-bylaws) Community Bylaws, the [Community Sovereignty Doctrine](/proposals/passed/2019-05-17_federation-sovereignty), and their RCOS-mapped artifacts — per FH1 Bylaws Art. 5 (decentralization review) and Art. 6 (bylaw amendment process)
2. Reviewing and admitting new members, per FH1 Bylaws Art. 11.03 and the Community Sovereignty Doctrine Art. II
3. Managing and reporting on the shared treasury, per FH1 Bylaws Art. 9 and the [Treasury Ruleset](/layers/3-economy/02-treasury-ruleset)
4. _<Reserved — operating and maintaining community platforms and websites is not yet named as a community responsibility in any ratified bylaws or doctrine. Pending future ratification.>_
5. Facilitating, supporting, and documenting developing Fruit Haven communities, per the Community Sovereignty Doctrine Art. V
6. Publishing learnings, failures, and adaptations openly, consistent with the transparency requirements of FH1 Bylaws Art. 9.02 and Art. 11.02
7. _<Reserved — stewardship of the Fruit Haven brand, public presence, and social media accounts is not yet named as a community responsibility beyond the "Fruit Haven" title itself (Community Sovereignty Doctrine Art. IV(b)). Pending future ratification.>_
8. _<Reserved — research and development of new approaches, tools, and methods, including software development, is not yet named as a community responsibility in any ratified bylaws or doctrine. Pending future ratification.>_

## Explicitly Out of Scope

:::rcos{clauses="2.2.3, 2.2.4, 2.2.5"}
:::

:::rationale{title="Why name what the community must not touch?"}
An unstated boundary is no boundary at all. Explicit out-of-scope items protect members from the community reaching into their private lives and finances, and protect independent communities from overreach.
:::

1. _<Reserved — exclusion of underlying server infrastructure, hosting accounts, and third-party service contracts is not yet stated in any ratified bylaws or doctrine. Pending future ratification.>_
2. Personal income, private finances, and private bank accounts of members, consistent with the Treasury Ruleset's distinction between treasury funds and personally-covered costs (FH1 Bylaws Art. 9)
3. Private relationships and personal living arrangements of members (except where safety-critical conditions apply as defined in Layer 4), per FH1 Bylaws Art. 8.01 (private homestead sovereignty)
4. Off-network projects and businesses that do not use Fruit Haven assets or operate under Fruit Haven governance, per FH1 Bylaws Art. 7.02(a) and the Community Sovereignty Doctrine Art. IV(a)
5. The internal governance of independent communities that have adopted RCOS — Fruit Haven stewards the standard, not the communities using it, per the [Federation Protocol](/layers/2-governance/04-federation-protocol) §1.1 and the Community Sovereignty Doctrine Art. III–V
6. Sovereign properties: Any property, should all the owners agree using the consensus method and the unanimity decision rule, may shed the title of "Fruit Haven" and effectively leave the project, per the Community Sovereignty Doctrine Art. IV(b)
