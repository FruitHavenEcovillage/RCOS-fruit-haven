# Proposal: Federation Sovereignty Doctrine — Strict Draft (Rejected)

- **Status:** Rejected
- **Submitted:** 2019-05-17
- **Submitted by:** Fruit Haven community members
- **Decision type:** Constitutional
- **Authorized decision path:** Consensus meeting under the FH1 Community Bylaws

---

## Summary

A stricter draft of the Federation Sovereignty Doctrine was put forward at the same 2019-05-17 community meeting that adopted the bylaws. It was debated alongside the version that ultimately passed (`proposals/passed/2019-05-17_federation-sovereignty.md`) and was rejected because it removed trustee override rights for legal-risk and property-value decisions, proposed a lower sovereignty threshold, granted automatic residency rights (rather than access rights) across communities, and kept the "Community Sovereignty Doctrine" and "Sovereignty and Jurisdiction Doctrine" as two separate documents rather than merging them into a single charter.

## Detailed Diff — What This Draft Proposed vs. What Was Adopted

### 1. Absolute External Sovereignty (Art. 6.02(c) of Bylaws → §6 of adopted protocol)

**This draft proposed:**
> The community declares full sovereignty and does not recognise the claim or jurisdiction of any foreign organisation or law, including decisions by individual trustees, that would override community governance on any matter within the community area.

**What was adopted instead (§4.3 of federation-protocol.md):**
> This charter shall not override the authority of property owners when addressing legal risk, removing a person due to legal risk, making decisions that significantly affect property value, or making decisions that significantly affect legal liability.

**Why it was rejected:**
The strict draft would have removed trustee override rights entirely within the community area. Trustees objected that without these carve-outs they would bear legal and financial liability for decisions they had no authority to reverse. The community bylaws themselves already acknowledged the trustee legal contract as the supreme document (Art. 6.02(a)), making the absolute sovereignty clause internally contradictory. The adopted version restores trustee authority specifically for legal-risk and property-value matters while leaving day-to-day community governance with the community.

---

### 2. Two-Document Hierarchy (Bylaws Art. 6.02(a) → §6 of adopted protocol)

**This draft proposed:**
Maintain two distinct sovereignty documents as separate authoritative instruments:
1. *Fruit Haven Community Sovereignty Doctrine* — governing community-area autonomy and member rights.
2. *Fruit Haven Sovereignty and Jurisdiction Doctrine* — governing cross-community federation rules and the relationship to the trustee legal contract.

Community bylaws would be subordinate to both, via the chain:  
`FH1 Trustee Legal Contract > FH Community Sovereignty Doctrine > FH1 Trustee Bylaws > FH Sovereignty & Jurisdiction Doctrine > FH1 Community Bylaws`

**What was adopted instead:**
A single merged charter (the Federation Protocol) covering both community autonomy and cross-community federation rules, with one clear supremacy clause (§6).

**Why it was rejected:**
Maintaining two separate documents was judged to create ambiguity about which applied in edge cases, and to require maintaining two ratification records. The merged approach was simpler, more coherent, and easier for new members to understand.

---

### 3. Lower Sovereignty Threshold (→ §3.3 of adopted protocol)

**This draft proposed:**
A community area achieves sovereignty when:
- It has at least **one resident Fruit Haven member** and **one additional resident** (total: 2); AND
- It has formally ratified written bylaws.

**What was adopted instead (§3.3):**
- At least **one resident Fruit Haven member and two additional residents** (total: 3); AND
- Formally ratified written bylaws; AND
- Those bylaws are displayed within the community area and available in Fruit Haven archives.

**Why it was rejected:**
A two-person threshold was considered too low to constitute a genuine "community" capable of stable self-governance. With only two residents, any disagreement would produce an immediate deadlock. The adopted threshold of three provides a minimum quorum for the consensus process and ensures a community has enough social mass before it acquires full autonomous jurisdiction.

---

### 4. Automatic Cross-Community Residency Rights (→ §2.1 of adopted protocol)

**This draft proposed:**
Any recognised Fruit Haven member automatically gains **residency rights** in any Fruit Haven community area upon arrival, unless a formal behavioral restriction is in place.

**What was adopted instead (§2.1–2.2):**
Fruit Haven members and residents gain **access rights** to any community area (not residency rights), unless a behavioral restriction applies. Access does not automatically grant residency or regular-use rights.

**Why it was rejected:**
Automatic residency rights would have bypassed each community's right to manage its own residential population (§1.1). A sovereign community could have been obligated to provide ongoing accommodation to any federation member regardless of capacity or compatibility. The adopted version preserves access (so members are not excluded) while leaving residency decisions in the hands of each sovereign community.

---

### 5. Federation Exit Mechanism (→ §4.2 of adopted protocol)

**This draft proposed:**
A Fruit Haven property may leave the federation by a **simple majority vote** of its trustees, with 30 days notice to the wider federation.

**What was adopted instead (§4.2):**
A property may leave only if **all owners consent** through the consensus process using the **unanimity decision rule**.

**Why it was rejected:**
A simple majority exit mechanism would allow a slim majority of trustees to strip minority-interest owners of federation membership against their will. The unanimity rule protects all co-owners and ensures that exit is a genuine collective decision rather than a faction decision.

## Rationale (as submitted)

The strict draft argued that the community area should be fully self-governing within its boundaries, and that trustee legal-risk carve-outs gave too much unilateral power to individual trustees to override community consensus decisions. It also argued that a lower sovereignty threshold would encourage faster formation of new communities within the network.

## Why Rejected

The draft failed to reach consensus at the 2019-05-17 community meeting for the following combined reasons:

1. **Internal contradiction:** The community bylaws already placed the FH1 Trustee Legal Contract at the top of the document hierarchy (Art. 6.02(a)). A clause in the same bylaws declaring those legal-contract rights void within the community area was self-contradicting and unenforceable.
2. **Trustee liability exposure:** Removing property-value and legal-risk carve-outs would have left trustees legally exposed to consequences they had no recognised authority to prevent.
3. **Operational fragility:** The two-person sovereignty threshold and automatic residency rights were judged likely to create governance crises in small or newly formed communities.
4. **Document complexity:** Maintaining two separate sovereignty instruments added administrative overhead without substantive benefit.

The adopted version (`proposals/passed/2019-05-17_federation-sovereignty.md`) resolved all four concerns while preserving community autonomy over day-to-day operations and residency decisions.
